abstract: 'Background: For a safe return to sport (RTS) after an anterior cruciate
  ligament reconstruction (ACLR), full recovery of hamstrings and quadriceps strength
  and restoration of the hamstrings-to-quadriceps strength ratio (H/Q ratio), is a
  common concern. Over the last decades, toque-angle profiles and angle-specific H/Q
  ratio profiles have received an increasing amount of interest. The evolution of
  these angle-specific strength analyses after RTS is unknown. The objectives of this
  study were&amp;nbsp;to identify any deficits or asymmetries in hamstrings and quadriceps
  torque-angle profiles and the angle-specific H/Q ratio profile in ACLR athletes
  at the time of RTS and&amp;nbsp;to assess if asymmetries persist&amp;nbsp;6 months
  following RTS.&lt;br&gt;&lt;br&gt;Methods: Twenty athletes with ACLR (hamstrings
  tendon graft) performed isokinetic strength tests for concentric knee flexion and
  extension (60°/s) at the moment of RTS, 3 months post RTS and 6 months post RTS.
  Twenty uninjured control participants were tested once.&lt;br&gt;&lt;br&gt;Results:
  Angle-specific analyses and peak torques showed lower hamstrings strength in the
  injured leg of ACLR athletes compared to their uninjured leg at RTS. Furthermore,
  angle-specific analyses showed a lower hamstrings strength and H/Q ratio in the
  injured leg compared to controls at larger knee flexion angles. The latter deficit
  was not identified with a peak-based analysis. The asymmetries identified at RTS
  did not change over the 6 months following RTS.&lt;br&gt;&lt;br&gt;Conclusions:
  Athletes with ACLR show strength deficits and asymmetries that persist even 6 months
  after RTS. As some asymmetries may go undetected by peak-based analyses, angle-specific
  analyses need to be monitored as well.'
