abstract: ABSTRACTThis study described the recovery responses following match play
  and examined the effects of manipulating training load 48 h post‐match in Italian
  Serie A youth soccer players. Forty‐eight players were assessed using the countermovement
  jump (CMJ), isometric posterior‐chain muscle test (IPC), muscle soreness and fatigue
  before (pre) and after (0.5 h post, 48 h post and 72 h post) a match. At 48 h post‐match,
  players were randomly assigned to a complete training (CT; n = 26) or a reduced
  training (RT; n = 22) group. Recovery differences were analysed between time points
  and training groups, with training loads quantified on match day (MD) and match
  day plus two (MD + 2). Recovery measures were impaired immediately post‐match (p &lt; 0.05).
  IPC and muscle soreness demonstrated incomplete recovery 48 h post‐match (p &lt; 0.05),
  whereas CMJ and fatigue returned to baseline (p &gt; 0.05). Training load on MD
  did not differ between groups (p &gt; 0.05), whereas the CT group had higher load
  on MD + 2 compared to RT (p &lt; 0.05). A significant time × group interaction was
  observed for CMJ height and IPC measures (p &lt; 0.05), with reductions in physical
  performance observed in the CT group from 48 to 72 h post‐match (p &lt; 0.05). A
  youth soccer match acutely impaired physical performance and recovery status, with
  prolonged and incomplete recovery of hamstring force and elevated muscle soreness
  48 h post‐match. A high‐volume and high‐intensity session administered 48 h post‐match
  negatively influenced physical performance compared to a moderate training session.
